From Norfolk and Manchester to Wexford: The McCleane-Halligan Family

Two ancestral routes drawn from the consolidated research dossier, for the McCleane Gang of Casa-Rio. Every link carries the confidence label it was given in the research. Nothing here has been promoted from probable to proven. Collateral families branch sideways and are never placed in the direct line.

Known contradictions and cautions
  1. Alexander Todd's birthplace. One earlier working summary said Cambridgeshire, but the expanded reconstruction records March 1878 in Liverpool. Liverpool is used here provisionally and the birth certificate remains the arbiter.
  2. Elizabeth Maher. Probably the mother of John McClean, but the 1846 Catholic ceremony occurred eleven years after John's birth. She may instead have been a stepmother, or the ceremony may have been a delayed solemnisation.
  3. William and Mary McClean. Retained as highly probable parents of James, exactly as agreed, never as confirmed.
  4. Margaret McCleane. Maiden name remains unknown. It has not been invented here.
  5. Peggy McCleane. The evidence supports Margaret as her formal name and Peggy as the family nickname.
  6. Tony Halligan. Not expanded to Anthony without a registration.
  7. William Halligan's parents. James and Mary of James's Street are an attractive match but still candidates.
  8. Washington siblings. John, Bathsheba Proctor and Susanna Kimm remain public-tree claims.
  9. Manchester cluster. Shared surname, religion and associates do not by themselves prove sibling relationships.
  10. Muchwood and South Main Street McCleanes. Plausible Wexford collateral branches, but not yet linked to the High Street household.

Railway migration is the strongest single theme. John Todd, George and Elijah Sewell, Alexander and Frederick Todd, William Halligan and Philip Wickham junior were all employed in railway-related work. On the McCleane side the recurring pattern is skilled urban trades: plumbing, painting, fitting, teaching and dressmaking.

Spelling variants to preserve in all searching
  • McClean, McCleane. The Manchester records use McClean; the Wexford records settle on McCleane. Bassett's 1885 directory also gives M'Clean and Jno. M'Clean.
  • Clowery, Clowry, Clowrey. All three appear for the same Wexford family.
  • Donohoe, Donoghue, Donohue, Donoughue. All four appear for Mary Anne, wife of John Clowery.
Recommended research when the project resumes

  2. Establish John McClean's movement from Manchester to Wexford using marriage, trade directory and death material.
  3. Search earlier St Chad and surrounding Catholic registers for a marriage or baptism connecting James to William and Mary.
  4. Obtain William Halligan and Polly Todd's full 1926 marriage entry, and William's Dublin birth or baptism, to test the James's Street family.
  5. Verify William Sanctuary and Elizabeth Gent's marriage and Bathsheba's baptism in the Hilgay and Gayton parish registers.
  6. Verify the baptisms and marriages of John Washington, Bathsheba Proctor and Susanna Kimm before expanding their descendants.
  7. Identify the two deceased Todd children and the missing Wickham children.
